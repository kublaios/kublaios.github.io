Career coaching is expensive, at least on paper. I'm not here to tell you whether it's worth it. Not here to sell you coaching either. Just passing on my experience as a coachee.

## 1. Before everything, get to know yourself

A catchy title for this one would be *"Know thyself",* but not this time.

*If I were to know myself, why would I need advice?* The point here is to describe yourself and your aspirations to someone else to aid the journey you are about to embark on.

Do you have a vision for your career in mind? It has to be on paper. What about your core values? Write them down. What were the outcomes of your last performance review? Your recently-identified improvement areas? Anything that could give you and your coach a head start, put them into words and share the result with your coach. They will thank you.

*Vision? What's vision? My core values!? Where did that come from??* That's me on day one. Ask these questions to yourself before someone else does. Why are they important? A topic for another day.

Tip: Give the [16Personalities](https://www.16personalities.com/) test a go. It's a well-recognized tool that can assess your personality and describe it non-judgmentally.

## 2. Set goals, really

As clichè as it sounds, you need goals. They are a compass to your journey. *Easy*, you may think, *I could find several in an instant*. Correct, you wouldn't be here if you didn't want to change some things. That's the challenge: As you define your goals, you'll realize you need to let some of them go because there are just so many things to focus on. Good luck.

When defining your goals, ask yourself three questions: On a scale of 1 to n, where you are, where you want to be, and how you will know you've reached your destination. The last one is not easy to tell, but it's crucial. You can materialize it with key results.

Do not forget that goals are useless unless you keep them in sight. Check them regularly, ideally with your coach.

## 3. Demand. From both sides.

Prepare for every session. Imagine your coach asking *"What would you like to talk about today?"* Write down your answer but don't stop there - send it to your coach. Not only you're prepared now, but you've demanded your coach's attention and given them a chance to prepare, too. The session will be more productive and focused.

Set yourself action items. Hold yourself accountable. Show your coach how much you care about your progress. You are paying for this, remember?

## 4. Take notes (duh)

It's a no-brainer, I know. Be thorough with your note-taking. Don't limit it to takeaways during the session. Make a journal. Write for your future self. Write as if you have to convince yourself that it was worth it. It's the only way to remember and appreciate the valuable, little things.

## 5. Be prepared for uncomfortable conversations

The coaches love digging into negative emotions. They challenge you for the things you take for granted. They show you the flaws in your logic or behavior. Get ready for that. It's not personal, it's professional. It's for your good.

## 6. The key benefit is a new perspective

You are not paying for a mentor. Your coach will never tell you what to do. Instead, they will ask you questions that you never thought of. You will gain a new angle, perspective, and a holistic view of your situation. Appreciate it, and make your value assessment based on this.

## 7. Don't expect miracles

Sometimes the solution is annoyingly simple. What matters is that there's a solution, regardless of whether it's simple or complex. Maybe it took time to get there, but it's there. You have a plan, it's time to execute it.

## 8. If you're willing, anyone can be your coach

This one is not about picking a coach, or any coach. It's about applying the patterns between you and a coach to the rest of your life. Find people who challenge you, ask you uncomfortable questions, and elevate you and your perspective. If you surround yourself with such people, you get the benefits of coaching for free.

## 9. Be proud of yourself

You decided to fight against inertia. You decided to be challenged. You decided to change. Remind yourself of that and be proud. Growth is not felt in a day. Keep going.
