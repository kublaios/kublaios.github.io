I'll start with a confession. When announcing this page on LinkedIn, I asked for help from AI (confession over). It aptly described my writing cadance as "irregularly regular". I have to admit — it is aging like fine wine.

On the other hand, I have less of an irregular cadance when it comes to reading tech newsletters. With tech, I mean, content from tech folks, not necessarily technical material. Some of them write about leadership, some others on life, some on growth (hi!) and so on.

Apart from being *techy guys*, they have another thing in common: They all (try to) stick to a schedule. I came to notice that this leads to some antipatterns. I want to list some of those.

#### 1. **Vowed to publish *something* whatever it takes**

As you probably know, this is the most common among writers. In my opinion, every once in a while is okay, too. But unfortunately, this can get addictive, easily. Imagine a writer you follow begins occassionally posting excerpts from what they read and adds their two cents to still make it somehow *their* content. When *occassionally*, that's okay, right? What about when it becomes a habit and makes you forget whose work it was you came to read in the first place?

#### 2. **Cross-posts like there's no tomorrow**

You follow a content creator on one medium (say, on YouTube) and you subscribe to their newsletter because you like their content. Only to discover what they publish is simply the transcripts of their YouTube videos. Duh.

#### 3. **Sorry-not-sorry but still kinda sorry**

This is when the author gets apologetic (because they don't publish as often, or they don't stick to their usual format) and sometimes tries not to make it like so. I know this from the first hand but over time, I started to find it pointless - if someone is reading your work, you didn't lose them. And if you would without an apology, maybe the damage was already done?

#### 4. **Please engage before the algorithm hits**

There are several forms of this. The simplest, sometimes naive, sometimes sneaky one is making it look like handing the mic over to the reader. Say, the post is about the author's favorite cities, they finish with "But enough about me - what about your favorite cities? Let me know in the comments!" The Oscar is not going to this one for sure. The more sneaky (or even, fraudulent) form of this is what's defined as "clickbait" or "[ragebait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rage-baiting)". Thankfully, none of the authors I follow do the latter - and the former is, at least for me, not a big deal if it's once in a blue moon.

When I was triggered to write this I only had two, but the writing magic happened and helped me complete my list. I could go ahead and ask AI to add a few more but the merit of our work in the new era will be the humane parts. Let's not go there now, though.

But enough about me - what about your observations? What don't you like when reading others' work? Let me know in the comments!
