In his podcast with Lex Fridman, Jeff Bezos touches on how today's technology is shortening our attention spans:

---

*Jeff Bezos ([01:42:20](https://youtube.com/watch?v=DcWqzZ3I2cY&t=6140)): We co-evolve with our tools. So we invent new tools and then our tools change us.*

*Lex Fridman: And there's some aspect, even just inside business, where you don't just make the customer happy, but you also have to think about where is this going to take humanity if you zoom out a bit?*

*JB: A hundred percent and you can feel your brain. Brains are plastic and you can feel your brain getting reprogrammed. (...) But that happens with everything. I think we still have yet to see the full repercussions of this, I fear, but I think one of the things that we've done online and largely because of social media is we have trained our brains to be really good at processing super short form content. Your podcast flies in the face of this. You do these long format things.*

*LF: Books do too.*

*JB: And reading books is a long format, and if something is convenient, we do more of it. We carry around in our pocket a phone, and one of the things that phone does for the most part is it is an attention shortening device because most of the things we do on our phone shorten our attention spans. And I'm not even going to say we know that that's bad, but I think it's happening. That's one of the ways we're co-evolving with that tool. But I think spending some of your time and some of your life doing long attention span things is important.*

---

We're in the same boat if you think there is nothing particularly interesting about this observation. What's interesting for me, however, is the idea of using (book) reading as an attention-span-lengthening tool.

When reflecting on my struggles with not reading (books) as much as I'd like to, concentration is easily the biggest one. But the real damage is not how I end up with a single digit on my yearly reading stats; it's how I am inclined towards shorter forms of content more and more.

Do you see the vicious cycle here? I prefer short content because my attention span is not enough for long content, and my attention span gets even shorter because I don't consume long content. So, the antidote to not reading books due to being unable to concentrate is reading more books (so you are able to concentrate). And this is only a side-effect. The real benefit is the longer attention span. Did you need something else?
